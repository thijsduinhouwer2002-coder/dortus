Handling phishing reports in Jira Service Management

Diese Anleitung auf Deutsch

"Is this email legit?" is one of the most common requests a service desk gets. This page covers how those reports arrive in JSM, how to look at the suspicious email safely, and what to check before you answer.

How phishing reports arrive on a request

Most teams route reports into JSM through its email channel. Three patterns are common:

  • Forward as attachment. The user forwards the suspicious email to the service desk address as an attachment. The original arrives on the request as a .eml file, or, when it goes through Jira's mail handler, as a file without an extension.
  • Report button in Microsoft 365. If your tenant sends user-reported messages to a custom mailbox and that mailbox is the JSM email channel, every report becomes a request with the original message attached. Setup details below.
  • Drag and drop. An agent or user drags the message from Outlook onto an existing request. Classic Outlook produces a .msg file, new Outlook and Mac clients produce .eml.

Forwarding inline (the text pasted into the body) is the one pattern to discourage: it drops the headers, which are exactly what you need to judge the email.

Report buttons: Microsoft 365 and KnowBe4

Many companies give employees a "report phishing" button in Outlook and want every report to become a JSM request. That works, but each button has its own rules for where reports can go.

Microsoft Defender for Office 365. In the user reported settings, an admin sends reports to Microsoft, to a reporting mailbox, or to both. That mailbox must be an Exchange Online mailbox. Distribution groups, external addresses and on-premises mailboxes are not allowed, so you cannot enter the JSM project's Atlassian address there. The route is: reporting mailbox first, then connect that mailbox to JSM or let Exchange redirect its mail to the project's address. Our Microsoft 365 mailbox guide covers both. Microsoft also asks you to mark it as a SecOps mailbox and exclude it from data loss prevention.

Per Microsoft's Report button page, a message reported as phishing is deleted from the user's mailbox. A delegate reporting from a shared mailbox needs Send As permission, or the report never reaches the reporting mailbox.

KnowBe4 Phish Alert Button. In the button's settings, the field "Send Non-Simulated Emails to:" takes one or more addresses, separated by commas. Reported KnowBe4 simulations are not forwarded, so training campaigns stay out of the queue. For the EXE and Hybrid buttons, "Email Format" sets whether the original goes as an EML or MSG attachment, and a "Forwarded Email Prefix" goes in front of the original subject. Test with one report before you rely on it.

Both together. Microsoft's settings also accept reports from non-Microsoft buttons. Its rules: the original is attached unmodified as an uncompressed .EML or .MSG file, the subject starts with 3| or Phishing: for phishing, and the report comes from the user who received the email, not a service account. Microsoft advises against running its button and another one side by side, because duplicate reports create noise.

Microsoft's pages do not describe how its own button packages a report in the mailbox, so look at a test report first. Once the original is on the request as a .eml or .msg file, the next section applies; the part about MailView covers reading it there.

Looking at the email without opening it in Outlook

JSM cannot show .eml or .msg attachments; it offers them as a download. The habit that follows, download and open in Outlook, is the one thing phishing training tells people not to do: it can load remote tracking images, render active content and make the links one accidental click away. Safer options, in order:

  1. Read the raw .eml in a text editor. Safe, but the headers are hard to read and the body is often base64 or HTML soup.
  2. Use a viewer that renders the email with scripts, forms and remote images stripped, and that shows the headers in plain words. Inside JSM is best, so the triage happens on the request and the answer goes back through the same ticket.

What to check

Authentication results. The receiving mail server records three checks in the headers. SPF: was the sending server allowed to send for that domain? DKIM: was the message signed by the domain and unchanged since? DMARC: does the visible From address line up with those two, and what did the domain owner ask to happen on failure? A fail on all three is a strong signal; a pass on all three does not prove the email is safe, only that it really came from that domain. The email check on this site shows these results for a file or pasted headers, as the receiving server recorded them.

The From domain. Look for lookalikes: an extra letter, a swapped character, an accented letter that renders like the real one, or the brand name in a subdomain of an unrelated domain. Compare it with the Reply-To address, which attackers often point somewhere else.

Where links really go. The link text and the link target are two different things. Check the target of every link and button, and treat "the text says our portal, the target is somewhere else" as a red flag. When you paste a link into the ticket, defang it (hxxps://, dots in brackets) so nobody clicks it by accident.

Attachments inside the email. Note the names and types. Office files with macros, archives and HTML files deserve a sandbox, not a double-click.

Pressure. "Within 24 hours", "your mailbox will be suspended", "final notice". Urgency is the oldest trick and still the most common.

Answering and closing

Write the findings into the request in plain words so the reporter learns something: which checks failed, which domain was faked, where the link went. Keep the original attachment on the request; it is your evidence. If the email was genuine, say so and thank the reporter, because the next report from that person may be the one that matters.

Short answers

Can JSM preview a reported email? No. .eml and .msg attachments are downloads only.

Can the Microsoft Report button send straight to the JSM email address? No. Microsoft requires an Exchange Online mailbox and does not allow external addresses. Send reports to that mailbox, then connect it to JSM or redirect its mail to the project's address.

Is a DMARC pass enough to mark a report as safe? No. It shows the email came from the domain it claims. A compromised or newly registered domain passes just fine.

Should agents open reported emails in Outlook? Not as a routine. Use a text editor or a viewer that strips active content, and check the link targets before anyone clicks.
