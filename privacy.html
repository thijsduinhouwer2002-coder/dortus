Privacy policy

The Dortus apps for Confluence and Jira run on Atlassian Forge, inside Atlassian's cloud. Dortus has no servers for them and receives no app content. Below is what each app processes, where it is kept, and for how long.

Last updated: 30 October 2026

1. Who we are

Dortus ("we", "us") is an independent software vendor. We publish these apps for Atlassian Jira and Confluence:

  • MailView: .msg & .eml Viewer with Phishing Check for Jira (in this policy: MailView for Jira, or MailView)
  • MailView: .msg & .eml Viewer, Phishing Check for Confluence (MailView for Confluence)
  • Mermaid Editor for Confluence (formerly Mermaid Live Preview: diagrams as code for Confluence)
  • AskSpace Q&A: Questions and Answers for Confluence (AskSpace)
  • Live Countdown for Confluence (Live Countdown)
  • Mermaid Live Preview for Jira (Mermaid Live Preview for Jira)
  • Math for Confluence (Math for Confluence; submitted to the Marketplace, waiting for approval)

We also publish Arrears for Shopify, a separate product. It is described in section 14.

Contact for privacy questions and data requests: support@dortus.com

2. Our role

For business data inside our apps, you are the controller and Dortus is the processor. You decide which questions, diagrams, pages or attachments exist. We process that data only to deliver the features you switched on.

For your own contact with us (support email, billing questions), Dortus is the controller of that correspondence.

Short version. The Atlassian apps store nothing on Dortus servers, because there are none. MailView for Jira, MailView for Confluence, Live Countdown and Mermaid Live Preview for Jira have no app storage; Mermaid Live Preview for Jira keeps its diagrams on the Jira work item. AskSpace, Mermaid Editor for Confluence and Math for Confluence keep their data in Forge app storage in your Atlassian site. MailView for Jira counts how often it is used, as three numbers per site, through Atlassian. From version 1.2, Mermaid Editor for Confluence keeps similar plain counters about inserts and its feedback question.

3. The Dortus apps for Atlassian

3.1 Where the data lives

These apps are built on Atlassian Forge and run entirely inside Atlassian's cloud. AskSpace keeps its data in Forge app storage in your Atlassian site. Mermaid Editor for Confluence saves the diagram source in the macro on the Confluence page, and keeps version history in Forge app storage in your Atlassian site. Mermaid Live Preview for Jira saves diagrams on the Jira work item, as issue properties, and has no app storage. Math for Confluence saves the formula in the macro on the Confluence page, and keeps version history in Forge app storage in your Atlassian site. Live Countdown saves its settings in the macro on the Confluence page and has no app storage. MailView for Jira and MailView for Confluence have no app storage. To ask you once whether MailView works well for you, they keep one small entry in your browser's local storage, for the app's frame only: how many emails you have read in MailView (up to three), when the first was, how often the question was shown, and whether you answered it. It holds no account ID, no email content, subject, sender or file name, and not which answer you gave. It never leaves your browser, and clearing your browser data removes it. We do not operate an application server for these apps, and app content is not sent to Dortus.

These apps are built for Atlassian's "Runs on Atlassian" programme: there is no required data egress to Dortus for normal use of the apps.

One thing does reach us: Atlassian lets the developer of a Forge app see technical logs from the sites where it is installed, in Atlassian's developer console, for 30 days. AskSpace and MailView for Confluence only write error types, status codes, counts and timings to those logs, never your content, names or IDs. MailView for Jira writes no log lines in the published version. Live Countdown, Mermaid Live Preview for Jira and Math for Confluence write no log lines. Log sharing is on by default; your site's admins can turn it off.

MailView for Jira also keeps three plain counters through Atlassian's Forge metrics: how often the app loaded with at least one email, how often an email was opened, and how often "Copy findings" was used. These are numbers only, with no content, file names, work item keys or user names, and they exist so we can tell whether the app is used at all. Atlassian shows these counts to us per site where the app is installed, so we can see how often the app is used on your site, but nothing about what was in the emails.

From version 1.13, MailView for Jira and MailView for Confluence also keep plain counters for that feedback question: emails read, the question shown, Yes, Not really, closed, and the review or support page opened. AskSpace keeps plain counters the same way: questions asked, answers posted, and the same feedback counters. Only the name of the counter and +1 leave the browser, to Atlassian's platform. Your site admins can turn custom metrics off for the app in Atlassian Administration.

From version 1.2, Mermaid Editor for Confluence keeps plain counters the same way: how often a diagram is inserted, and how often the feedback question in the editor is shown, answered, closed, or leads to the review or support page. Only the name of the counter and +1 leave the browser, to Atlassian's platform. There is no diagram text, page, account ID or name in them. Atlassian shows these counts to us per site. Your site admins can turn custom metrics off for the app in Atlassian Administration.

3.2 What AskSpace processes

  • Question and answer text people enter, and the tags they add
  • Votes and best-answer marks
  • Space settings, for example who may ask, who may answer and who may moderate
  • Questions and answers imported from Questions for Confluence or a CSV file, and a record of each import so it can be undone
  • Atlassian account identifiers, needed to show authors and enforce permissions
  • Display names and avatar URLs provided by Atlassian APIs, so authors can be shown
  • In-app notification records, for example that someone answered your question
  • For the feedback question, per person: how many questions and answers they posted (up to three), when the first was, how often the question was shown, and whether they answered it; not which answer they gave

AskSpace reads Confluence content only to import questions from Questions for Confluence, when a space admin starts an import. It reads as that admin, so it sees only what they can see in Confluence, and it does not change anything in Questions for Confluence. Of what it reads, it keeps only the imported questions and answers, in Forge app storage.

3.3 What Mermaid Editor for Confluence processes

  • The diagram source you type in the macro
  • Earlier versions of that source (the last 20 saves per diagram), kept so you can step back after a bad edit
  • For each person who inserts diagrams: their Atlassian account ID, a count of successful inserts (it stops at 3), when their first insert happened, how often and when the app asked them for feedback (at most twice), and whether they answered or closed that question. Nothing else about the person is stored, and their answer is not stored

The diagram source is saved in the macro on the Confluence page. Earlier versions are kept in Forge app storage in your Atlassian site. The diagram is drawn in the browser of the person viewing the page. The app uses no external services. Apart from the plain counters described in section 3.1 (from version 1.2), nothing is sent to Dortus.

3.4 What MailView for Jira processes

MailView shows .eml and .msg email attachments on a Jira work item. When the panel opens, the app reads the work item's email attachments, and files without a known file type to see whether they are emails, from your Jira site in your browser, with your own Jira permissions, and renders the one you open there. The email, its headers, its inline images and the files inside it are processed only in your browser and only for as long as the panel is open.

  • MailView keeps no copies: it has no app storage and no server.
  • No email content is sent to Dortus or to any third party. The only thing that reaches Dortus are the three counters described in section 3.1, through Atlassian. The app makes no other outbound connections; images and other resources that an email tries to load from the internet are blocked.
  • Emails usually contain personal data (names, addresses, message text). That data stays in your Jira attachment, under your organisation's control, exactly as before you installed the app.
  • The app asks for one permission: read access to work items and their attachments. It cannot change, send or delete anything.
  • In Atlassian's development environment the app can show technical diagnostics to the developer (timings, status codes, sizes). These never contain email content, subjects or addresses, and they are not shown in the published version.

3.5 What MailView for Confluence processes

MailView for Confluence shows .eml and .msg email attachments on a Confluence page. It only reads the email attachments of the page you open, with your own Confluence permissions and with one permission for the app: read:attachment:confluence. The email is rendered in your browser and only for as long as it is open.

  • To find the emails, the app reads the list of attachments of that page. Files without a known file extension are opened only to check whether they are an email (the 10 newest). Files with a known extension, such as .pdf, and images are not opened.
  • When a page is exported to PDF or Word, the email chosen in the macro is read and turned into a static version inside Atlassian's Forge platform, with the permissions of the person who exports, and handed back to Confluence for the export file. Nothing is kept afterwards.
  • MailView for Confluence keeps no copies: it has no app storage and no server.
  • Nothing is sent to Dortus or to any third party. The app makes no outbound connections; images and other resources that an email tries to load from the internet are blocked.
  • Emails usually contain personal data (names, addresses, message text). That data stays in your Confluence attachment, under your organisation's control, exactly as before you installed the app.
  • The permission is read-only and covers attachments only. The app cannot read page text, and it cannot change, send or delete anything.
  • In Atlassian's development environment the app can show technical diagnostics to the developer (timings, status codes, sizes). These never contain email content, subjects or addresses, and they are not shown in the published version.

3.6 What Live Countdown for Confluence processes

  • The settings of each countdown: title, date, time, time zone, display options, and the text to show when the time is up

The settings are saved in the macro on the Confluence page, like the settings of any Confluence macro, so they follow the page's permissions and version history. The app has no app storage and asks for no permissions. It stores nothing about the people who add a countdown or read the page, and it uses no cookies or other storage in the browser. The countdown is worked out in the browser of the person viewing the page. The app makes no network calls, uses no external services, and nothing is sent to Dortus.

For a PDF or Word export, Confluence calls the app's export function on Atlassian's Forge platform with the macro's settings. The function returns a line of text for the export file. It calls nothing else and keeps nothing afterwards.

3.7 What Mermaid Live Preview for Jira processes

Mermaid Live Preview for Jira is a separate app from Mermaid Editor for Confluence (section 3.3).

  • The diagrams people add to a work item: for each diagram its ID, title and Mermaid code, and when it was created and last saved
  • Earlier versions of each diagram (up to 20): the code and when each version was saved
  • The order of the diagrams on the work item

All of this is stored on the Jira work item itself, as Jira issue properties in your Jira site. The app has no app storage, no backend and no database. It stores no account IDs, names or email addresses, unless someone types them into a diagram or a title.

Anyone who can see the work item can read its diagrams, in the app's panel or through Jira's REST API. Issue properties are not private to the app: other apps on your site that may read Jira work items can read them too.

The app asks for two permissions, read:jira-work and write:jira-work. It uses them in the browser, as the signed-in user, to read and save its own issue properties and to check whether that user may edit the work item. It talks only to your Jira. It makes no other network calls, uses no external services, and nothing is sent to Dortus.

3.8 What Math for Confluence processes

  • The LaTeX of each formula people type in the macro, and its display options (size and alignment)
  • Earlier versions of each formula (up to 20, at most 64 KB per formula): the LaTeX, the size and alignment, and when each version was saved

The formula is saved in the macro on the Confluence page, like the settings of any Confluence macro, so it follows the page's permissions and version history. Earlier versions are kept in Forge app storage in your Atlassian site, under the macro's ID. The app stores no account IDs, names or email addresses, unless someone types them into a formula.

The formula is drawn in the browser of the person viewing the page, with KaTeX and fonts that are part of the app. For a PDF or Word export, Confluence calls the app's export function on Atlassian's Forge platform with the formula. The function returns an image or text for the export file. It fetches nothing and keeps nothing afterwards.

The app asks for one permission, storage:app, for the version history. Atlassian tells the app whether its subscription on your site is active; the app stores nothing about that. It makes no network calls outside Atlassian, uses no external services, and nothing is sent to Dortus.

Only to provide the features you enable: storing and displaying questions and answers, rendering diagrams and formulas, showing countdowns, and showing email attachments. Our legal basis is the performance of our agreement with you, and our legitimate interest in keeping the service running securely.

7. Retention

  • AskSpace: content stays in Forge storage for your site. Uninstall and deletion follow Atlassian's platform rules for app storage.
  • Mermaid Editor for Confluence: the diagram source is part of the Confluence page, so it follows the page's own retention and deletion. Version history (the last 20 saves per diagram) and the feedback counter per account ID stay in Forge storage for your site. Uninstall and deletion follow Atlassian's platform rules for app storage.
  • Live Countdown: the settings are part of the Confluence page, so they follow the page's own retention and deletion. The app keeps nothing else.
  • Mermaid Live Preview for Jira: diagrams and their versions are part of the Jira work item, as issue properties, so they follow the work item's own retention and deletion. Deleting a diagram in the app removes it and its versions. Deleting the work item removes all of them. Issue properties are Jira data, not app storage, so we expect them to stay on the work items after an uninstall. This is not confirmed yet.
  • Math for Confluence: the formula is part of the Confluence page, so it follows the page's own retention and deletion. Version history (up to 20 saves per formula) stays in Forge storage for your site. Uninstall and deletion follow Atlassian's platform rules for app storage.
  • MailView: nothing is retained by Dortus or in Atlassian. Closing the work item is enough. The feedback entry described in section 3.1 stays in your browser until you clear it. The same goes for MailView for Confluence: closing the page is enough.
  • Support email: kept only as long as needed to help you and to keep a record of the issue.

8. Security

We are a small vendor and we do not claim certifications we do not have: if you need a security review or a data processing agreement, email us and we will answer concretely.

14. Arrears for Shopify (separate product)

Arrears runs on servers Dortus operates, unlike the Atlassian apps above. The general parts of this policy apply to Arrears as well. This section covers what is specific to Arrears.

14.1 What the app does

Arrears is an embedded Shopify app for business-to-business collections. It reads your companies, company locations, contacts, orders, and payment terms through the Shopify Admin API, and turns them into aging views, statements, reminders, payment allocations, and credit holds.

14.2 Where it runs

Unlike our Atlassian apps, Arrears needs a server. We host it on Fly.io in the Amsterdam region (EU), with the application database on a persistent volume attached to that machine. Access to the production environment is limited to Dortus.

14.3 What data Arrears processes and stores

  • Your shop domain and the Shopify access token issued during install
  • Company names, company locations, and payment terms as configured in Shopify
  • Company contact names and email addresses, used to address statements and reminders
  • Order references, amounts, currencies, due dates, and payment schedule status
  • Reminder records: recipient address, subject, message body, send time, and result
  • Payment records you enter, including allocation across open invoices
  • Credit limits, hold and release events, and the staff action behind them
  • App settings, such as sender name, dunning policy, and automation switches

Arrears does not ask for or store payment card details. Subscription charges are handled by Shopify billing.

We process this data only to show aging, produce statements, send the reminders you approve, allocate payments and apply credit holds, on the same legal basis as described in section 6.

14.4 Protected customer data

Business contact data (company contact names and email addresses) is used only to produce and send the statements and reminders you request, and to show you who was contacted. We do not use it for our own marketing, we do not sell it, and we do not share it with anyone outside the subprocessors listed in section 14.7.

14.5 Email sending

Statement and reminder emails are delivered through Resend. That means the recipient address, subject, and message body pass through Resend in order to be delivered, along with normal delivery metadata.

14.6 Shopify data requests and deletion

Arrears implements Shopify's mandatory compliance webhooks:

  • customers/data_request: we respond to the merchant with the data we hold for the requested person.
  • customers/redact: we delete or anonymise the stored personal data for that person.
  • shop/redact: we delete the shop's stored data after Shopify's redaction window.

Uninstalling the app also revokes our access: the stored session for your shop is removed when we receive the app/uninstalled webhook.

14.7 Subprocessors for Arrears

  • Shopify Inc.: platform, app hosting surface, and billing for Arrears.
  • Fly.io: application hosting and database storage for Arrears, EU (Amsterdam) region.
  • Resend: delivery of statement and reminder email from Arrears.

Arrears is hosted in the EU. Some of these subprocessors are established outside the EU and may process limited data there under their own transfer mechanisms.

14.8 Retention and security

Data is kept while the app is installed. It is deleted on shop redaction, and on request. Reminder history is kept so you can prove what was sent, until the shop data is deleted.

Traffic to Arrears is served over HTTPS only. Platform credentials and API keys are held as environment secrets, not in source code. Access to production is limited to Dortus.

14.9 Your rights

For data inside Arrears, start with the merchant that installed the app, or contact us directly.

14.10 The Arrears page on this website

The product walkthrough on the Arrears page is a YouTube embed served from youtube.com. It loads only when you open that page, and playing it means YouTube receives your IP address and player data under Google's own terms.
